I began gamedev at around 15, in 2018. Since then, I've made a lot of different projects, and while most of them ended up nowhere, there were a few that became playable little games I could share with my friends. I recently stumbled across a folder with some of these old projects, so I thought I'd share them here. 

They are not in any chronological (nor logical) order.

## SpaceMapper, a mix between trackmania and wipeout

This is one of if not my first ever "complete" game. I say that with quotes, since I was still missing some menus, polishing and bugfixing. But in essence, you could :
- play some skatepark maps to familiarize with the driving
- create your own maps in a map editor
- play those maps to get the fastest possible time
- host a multiplayer game and invite your friends to play your maps, with an ingame leaderboard

## A puzzle game about light rays

As I was playing a lot of puzzle games, I fell in love with The Talos Principle. Which led me to think: "I could make an awesome puzzle game too!". While I did, in fact, NOT make an awesome puzzle game, I still made a few level with my base idea, and I still think it could become a very cool game if further developped. The concept is that you will pay with light rays to fill up receptacles and activating mechanisms. 
You start with a laser gun, and it can reflect on surfaces like mirrors. Then, you'd have to deal with light filters, which only let some colors pass through, and different types of environments like glass or water which have different refraction indices, causing your light ray to bend, but also reflect on the surface. ## Do you know Tak? 

While reading the book The Name of The Wind from Patrick Rothfuss, I found out that he had made a board game from his fantasy world come to life in the real world. I asked my grandfather to build one for me since he loved wood work, and I played a lot of games with a friend. And as you can guess, I tried to make it virtual, so that we could play online. I found out halfway that there was already an online game available, so I didn't push the project all the way, but I was still able to play some games locally, and was very proud of it at the time.
